Thinking critically with AI

AI tools now draft emails, summarize documents and suggest decisions. Their answers are fluent and usually mostly right, which is why the wrong parts get through. This program gives people one habit for checking AI output, sized to what is at stake.

Format
50 min on demand, then a 3-hour workshop
Total time
3 h 50 min, including a 10-minute break
Who it is for
Anyone who uses AI tools at work, in any function. No technical background needed.
Delivery
In person or virtual, led by your own trainers or by us

Realistic by design

Every AI output in the exercises is mostly right, with two to four problems of the kind today's tools still make. One case has no problems at all, because rejecting good work also has a cost. Answer keys start with what the AI got right.

People leave using AI more, with better judgment about when to check and how deeply.

All materials are written in plain English for international teams, including people who work in a second language.

By the end, participants can

  • Explain why AI produces fluent but wrong answers, and name six common ways it fails.
  • Recognize the thinking traps that make a wrong output look right, such as automation bias and anchoring.
  • Size their checking effort to the stakes, using four tiers from Low to Critical.
  • Apply the CHECK method to find and fix the problems in an AI output from their own work.
  • Test claims against your organization's sources of record, using verification prompts.
  • Decide, document and disclose AI-assisted work in line with your AI policy.

The method

One habit, five steps

Everything in the program builds one habit, CHECK. The on-demand modules teach it, and the workshop repeats it on realistic cases until it becomes quick.

  1. C

    Consequence

    What happens if this is wrong, and who is affected?

    Pick the stakes tier. The tier sets how deeply you check.

  2. H

    Hunt the claims

    What exactly does this output say?

    List every fact, number, name, date, reference, promise and assumption.

  3. E

    Evidence

    Where is the source of record?

    Check each claim that matters. Mark what is right as well as what is wrong.

  4. C

    Challenge

    What is missing or one-sided? Is it exactly what I hoped to hear?

    Look for what the AI left out, what it added and what it could not know.

  5. K

    Keep ownership

    Would I sign this as my own work?

    Use, edit or discard. Most outputs need a small edit. For high-stakes work, record what you checked.

Stakes tiers

Check as deeply as the stakes require

Checking a personal note like a customer quote wastes the time AI saved. Checking a customer quote like a personal note lets errors reach customers. Four tiers set the minimum check before anything is used.

Low

Typical work
Personal notes, brainstorming, first drafts only you will see
Minimum check
Read it once to see if it makes sense.

Medium

Typical work
Internal summaries that other people act on
Minimum check
Check names, dates, decisions and numbers against the source.

High

Typical work
Customer emails, quotes, figures for leaders, public content
Minimum check
Check every claim against the source of record and recalculate the numbers. A second person reviews anything new.

Critical

Typical work
Safety instructions, binding contract terms, regulatory and compliance claims, decisions about people
Minimum check
AI is never the source. A qualified person signs off and records what was checked.

The tier depends on the consequence and the audience, not on the tool or on how hard the task is. The same task moves up a tier when it leaves the organization.
